# Course Review and Scope

## Review result

The repository was reviewed as a beginner-first Network+ N10-009 course, not only as a list of exam words. The active course now provides:

- An absolute-beginner path for computers, operating systems, number systems, Ethernet, switching, IPv4, ARP, TCP/UDP, NAT, and basic diagnostics.
- Dedicated teaching for all 25 N10-009 objectives from 1.1 through 5.5.
- A three-example workbook for every objective, with each evidence line or reasoning step explained.
- Guided labs, reproducible lab packs, packet captures, starter configurations, solutions, deliberate faults, PBQs, output-reading cases, subnetting practice, and four full practice exams.
- A practical bridge from Network+ knowledge into the first Cisco CLI, switching, routing, ACL, NAT, OSPF, IPv6, and verification skills needed before starting CCNA.

## What was already strong

The starting repository already had a sound structure, complete top-level objective coverage, careful vendor-neutral explanations, 26 guided labs, six lab packs, and extensive original practice. Important cautions were already present: NAT is not a firewall, ping is limited evidence, private addressing is not security, an STP blocking state is not automatically a fault, and real certificate validation should not be disabled as a shortcut.

## Gaps corrected in this review

The previous automated check mainly proved that required files and vocabulary existed. A word appearing somewhere did not prove that a beginner received a definition, examples, interpretation practice, and a check question. The review adds structural checks for the new objective workbook and CCNA bridge, and it keeps the original link, language, objective, exam, lab, and asset checks.

The previous study path also stopped at general job extensions. The CCNA readiness bridge now connects the vendor-neutral concepts to a coherent Cisco lab while clearly separating Network+ completion from CCNA mastery.

## What “complete” means here

“Complete” means that every published top-level N10-009 objective is mapped and taught at the stated Network+ depth, with review and practice. It does **not** mean:

- Passing the certification is guaranteed.
- Reading replaces hands-on practice or the experience CompTIA recommends.
- Every vendor command, product, or implementation is covered.
- The course replaces the current official CompTIA objectives, registration policy, or exam page.
- The CCNA bridge replaces a current CCNA course and its official objectives.

Exam providers can revise objective documents and policies. Check the official CompTIA page before booking an exam and the official Cisco learning path before starting CCNA.

## Validation boundary

The repository checker validates document structure, local links, objective sections, question/exam counts, lab inventory, selected teaching patterns, English-only active content, and required assets. It does not boot network operating systems or prove that every vendor/version accepts every example command.

Configuration must still be tested in the learner's chosen simulator or authorized lab. Commands can vary by IOS/IOS XE image, license, device family, and feature set. Packet captures in the repository are synthetic teaching assets. They prove that the files can be parsed and studied, not that a live multi-device topology was exercised during this documentation review.

## Recommended learner proof

A learner should not claim mastery from reading alone. Keep a portfolio containing:

1. A physical and logical diagram.
2. An address/VLSM plan.
3. Before-and-after command output for every major lab.
4. At least one packet walkthrough each for ARP/ICMP, DNS, TCP, and TLS.
5. One change plan with validation and rollback.
6. One troubleshooting ticket showing scope, theory, evidence, correction, verification, and prevention.
7. The complete CCNA entry-gate topology built without copying the solution.

That evidence is a much stronger readiness signal than a checklist of terms.
