# Behavioral and Communication Preparation

Use real authorized work or lab examples. Never invent production experience. A structured lab incident is acceptable when clearly described as a lab.

## STAR with technical evidence

- **Situation:** scope and user/business impact.
- **Task:** your responsibility and authority boundary.
- **Action:** evidence, reasoning, collaboration, change control, and communication.
- **Result:** measurable restoration, validation, prevention, and learning.

## Questions

1. Tell me about a difficult problem you isolated.
2. Describe a mistake and how you corrected/prevented it.
3. How do you communicate during an outage?
4. When did you escalate and why?
5. How do you prioritize several tickets?
6. How do you handle a frustrated user?
7. How do you learn an unfamiliar tool?
8. Describe a change with rollback.
9. How do you protect credentials and sensitive captures?
10. What lab/project best demonstrates your networking ability?

## Strong communication examples

Technical update:

> “Impact is limited to VLAN 20 across SW1–SW2. Local VLAN 20 traffic works; the trunk output omits VLAN 20 on SW2. I have recorded the baseline and prepared a one-line allowed-list correction with rollback. After approval I will verify trunk state, MAC learning, gateway access, and the application.”

Nontechnical update:

> “One department cannot reach the server across the building link. Other services remain available. We identified a configuration mismatch and have a low-risk correction ready for approval; the next update is in 15 minutes.”

Avoid unsupported certainty, blame, secret values, unexplained acronyms, and “fixed” without user-path validation.
